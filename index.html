Abstract: This paper develops a dynamic mechanism design framework where a principal seeks to elicit truthful information from agents operating in stochastic environments with initially unobservable state transition matrices. We prove that by structuring an indirect dynamic mechanism, the principal achieves identification of rational agent types over a finite horizon. An accompanying interactive Streamlit application provides computational verification of equilibria.

Research & Job Market Paper

Dynamic Mechanism Design, Industrial Organization & Infrastructure Economics

Job Market Paper (2026)

Identifying Rational Types in Unknown Environments under an Indirect Dynamic Mechanism

Author: Humberto Bernal, Ph.D.

Status: Under review at the International Journal of Game Theory

Core Contribution: When a principal interacts with agents over time, traditional dynamic screening models assume that the transition probability distribution of state variables is common knowledge. In many real-world environments—such as infrastructure regulation, procurement, and digital platforms—neither the type spaces nor state transitions are initially known.

This paper proves that an indirect dynamic mechanism can elicit truthful information revelation and identify rational behavior over finite horizons without triggering adverse selection failure. The theoretical results are complemented by an open-source computational application built with Streamlit, enabling researchers to simulate equilibria under stochastic shocks.
