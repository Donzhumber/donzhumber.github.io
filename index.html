Featured Job Market Paper & Research App

Identifying Rational Types in Unknown Environments under an Indirect Dynamic Mechanism (2026)

Status: Under review.

This work develops an indirect dynamic screening mechanism to elicit truthful type revelation without adverse selection failure, backed by an interactive computational simulation.

Abstract: This paper develops a dynamic indirect mechanism to identify rational types in unknown environments characterized by observational pooling. The central theoretical result, the Rational Type Identification Theorem, proves that the principal's Bayesian beliefs converge almost surely to the agent's true latent type along prolonged interactions without direct revelation reports. This asymptotic identification is operationalized by the Mano de Dios-Guadalupe process, an endogenous maximum-entropy noise kernel that ensures full support across actions within a competing-risks architecture. A complementary Implementability Theorem guarantees execution in Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium. Microeconometric calibration and deep-learning simulations of Colombian kidnapping validate rapid type separation and policy feasibility.

Keywords: indirect mechanism design, dynamic screening, endogenous implementation noise, Bayesian learning, type identification • JEL: C73, D82, K42

Current Active Research Agendas (2026)

1. AI, Automation & Labor Market Transitions in Colombia (2022–2030)

Evaluates the structural impact of Artificial Intelligence, LLMs, and automation on Colombia's labor market by integrating GEIH and ECV microdata (2022–2025) via statistical matching and micro-imputation. Grounded in the synthetic cohort pseudo-panel methodology of Deaton (1985), and occupational exposure indices developed by Felten et al. (2021), Webb (2020), and Eloundou et al. (2023), this research assesses empirical shocks on unemployment, the informal labor market—measured by the lack of monetary pension contributions—and wages, projecting prospective scenarios for 2026–2030.

2. Dynamic Asymmetric Information, Adverse Selection & Moral Hazard in Labor Contracts

Investigates dynamic labor interactions plagued by asymmetric information, adverse selection, and moral hazard in unknown environments. Applying the Rational Type Identification Theorem, this agenda develops indirect dynamic screening mechanisms that ensure almost-sure Bayesian belief convergence to agents' latent productivity types along prolonged interactions, preventing adverse selection market breakdown without requiring direct type revelation.

Humberto Bernal
Working Paper (2026), under review

Formulates an indirect dynamic screening mechanism under asymmetric information where state transitions and agent type spaces are initially unknown, proving truthful revelation without adverse selection failure.

Abstract: This paper develops a dynamic mechanism design framework where a principal seeks to elicit truthful information from agents operating in stochastic environments with initially unobservable state transition matrices. We prove that by structuring an indirect dynamic mechanism, the principal achieves identification of rational agent types over a finite horizon. An accompanying interactive Streamlit application provides computational verification of equilibria.

Teaching Portfolio & Dossier

Core and Advanced University Courses in Microeconomics, Game Theory, and Global Economics

Graduate Programs

Advanced Microeconomics
Master's Program

Rigorous mathematical treatment of modern microeconomic theory, formalizing consumer choice through duality, expenditure minimization, and indirect utility systems under certainty and risk. Axiomatic analysis of producer theory, cost function structures, profit maximization, and competitive partial and general equilibrium theorems. In-depth investigation of information economics, contract theory, and asymmetric information, modeling adverse selection, moral hazard, and signaling mechanisms. Formalization of dynamic mechanism design, social choice theory, and market failure solutions across non-competitive market environments. Students develop computational simulations and solve complex analytical problem sets bridging theory with empirical industrial regulation.

Global Markets

Advanced analysis of international product and factor markets, global production networks, and cross-border commercial strategy. Examines tariff and non-tariff barriers, exchange-rate pass-through mechanisms, strategic trade policies, and foreign direct investment (FDI) decisions. Evaluates market entry structures, multinational corporate behavior, cross-border pricing strategies, and global value chain integration. Analyzes multilateral governance frameworks, free trade agreements, and macroeconomic shock transmissions across developing and emerging economies. Undergraduate Programs

Game Theory
Undergraduate

Comprehensive study of interactive decision theory, formalizing strategic behavior across non-cooperative games in normal and extensive forms. Derivation and application of solution concepts: Nash Equilibrium in pure and mixed strategies, Subgame Perfection (SPE), and backward induction algorithms. Rigorous analysis of incomplete information games: Bayesian Nash Equilibrium and Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium applied to signaling, screening, and adverse selection. Strategic applications to industrial organization, oligopolistic pricing (Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg), bargaining protocols, and auction design. Hands-on computational exercises and interactive Python/Streamlit simulations enabling students to model equilibrium dynamics and counter-strategies.

Economic Growth & Development
Undergraduate

In-depth exploration of long-run economic expansion and structural transformation, starting from the neoclassical Solow-Swan framework and convergence hypotheses. Formalization of endogenous growth models: human capital accumulation (Lucas), R&D-driven technological innovation (Romer), and creative destruction (Aghion-Howitt). Analysis of structural development constraints, credit market imperfections, poverty traps, wage polarization, and income inequality dynamics. Investigation of institutional economics: property rights enforcement, governance quality, political economy frictions, and colonial origins of comparative development. Integration of applied microeconometrics and quasi-experimental methods to evaluate real-world public policy impacts and infrastructure interventions.

International Economics
Undergraduate

Rigorous foundations of classical, neoclassical, and modern international trade theories, including Ricardian comparative advantage and the Heckscher-Ohlin-Samuelson theorem. Analysis of New Trade Theory models: monopolistic competition, consumer love-of-variety preferences (Krugman), and heterogeneous-firm trade models (Melitz). Economic evaluation of commercial policy instruments: welfare impacts of ad-valorem tariffs, import quotas, export subsidies, and anti-dumping regulations. International macroeconomics: balance of payments accounting, nominal and real exchange rate determination, purchasing power parity (PPP), and uncovered interest parity (UIP). Critical assessment of trade agreements, global supply chain disruptions, geopolitical trade tensions, and regional economic integration.

Introduction to Economics
Undergraduate

Foundational exposition of fundamental microeconomic and macroeconomic principles for university students across economic and social disciplines. Microeconomic fundamentals: scarcity, opportunity cost, supply-demand interactions, market equilibrium price determination, and price elasticities. Analysis of welfare economics, market efficiency, government price controls, monopoly market distortions, externalities, and public goods provision. Macroeconomic foundations: national income accounting (GDP), price index measurement (inflation), labor market dynamics (unemployment), and business cycles. Introduction to macroeconomic policy tools: central bank monetary policy mechanisms, interest rate setting, fiscal spending, and public deficit sustainability.
